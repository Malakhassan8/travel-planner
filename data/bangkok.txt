CITY: Bangkok, Thailand

## Understand
Bangkok is Thailand's capital, a huge, energetic city known for ornate temples, street food, markets, and a mix of old-world canals and modern skyscrapers. It's one of the best-value major cities in the world for travelers, with excellent food and transport at low prices.

## Get in
Suvarnabhumi (BKK) and Don Mueang (DMK) are the main airports; both connect to the city via train, bus, or taxi. Bangkok is also a common overland/train hub for onward travel across Thailand and to neighboring countries.

## Get around
The BTS Skytrain and MRT subway are clean, cheap, and avoid Bangkok's notorious traffic — use them whenever a route is covered. Tuk-tuks are fun but should be price-negotiated beforehand; metered taxis are cheap if the driver uses the meter. Longtail boats and the Chao Phraya river ferry are practical for river-adjacent sights.

## See
- Grand Palace and Wat Phra Kaew (Temple of the Emerald Buddha): the top must-see, strict dress code (covered shoulders/knees) enforced.
- Wat Arun (Temple of Dawn): striking riverside temple, especially photogenic at sunset from across the river.
- Wat Pho: home of the giant reclining Buddha, also a birthplace of traditional Thai massage.
- Chatuchak Weekend Market: massive market, thousands of stalls, weekends only.
- Chinatown (Yaowarat): street food destination, especially in the evening.
- Jim Thompson House: teak house museum showcasing Thai silk history and traditional architecture.

## Do
- Take a Thai cooking class, widely available and popular with travelers.
- Traditional Thai massage, very affordable compared to Western prices, widely available and generally high quality.
- Chao Phraya river cruise, especially dinner cruises with city skyline views.
- Day trip to the Damnoen Saduak floating market or Ayutthaya's ancient temple ruins.
- Rooftop bars (e.g., in Sathorn/Silom) for skyline views, mid-range splurge.

## Eat
- Street food is central to Bangkok's food culture: pad thai, som tam (papaya salad), mango sticky rice, boat noodles — all cheap and widely available.
- Night markets (Rot Fai Market, Chinatown) combine food stalls with shopping.
- Mid-range Thai restaurants are inexpensive by Western standards; upscale dining (including Michelin-starred street food stalls) is still a good value.
- Tap water isn't drinkable; bottled or filtered water is cheap and everywhere.

## Drink
- Beer (Chang, Singha) is cheap and widely available.
- Thai iced tea is a popular non-alcoholic option.
- Rooftop bars offer higher-end cocktails at a premium relative to street prices, but still cheaper than equivalent Western cities.

## Sleep
- Budget: hostels and guesthouses, especially around Khao San Road, 8-15 USD/night.
- Mid-range: 3-4 star hotels, 30-60 USD/night.
- Luxury: 5-star riverside hotels, 100-200+ USD/night.

## Budget
Bangkok is very budget-friendly: a traveler can manage comfortably on 25-35 USD/day including guesthouse, street food, BTS/MRT transport, and a couple of paid attractions. Mid-range comfort runs 50-80 USD/day.
