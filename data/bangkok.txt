CITY: Bangkok, Thailand

## Understand
Bangkok is Thailand's capital, a huge, energetic city known for ornate temples, street food, markets, and a mix of old-world canals and modern skyscrapers. It's one of the best-value major cities in the world for travelers, with excellent food and transport at low prices.

## Get in
Suvarnabhumi (BKK) and Don Mueang (DMK) are the main airports; both connect to the city via train, bus, or taxi. Bangkok is also a common overland/train hub for onward travel across Thailand and to neighboring countries.

## Get around
The BTS Skytrain and MRT subway are clean, cheap, and avoid Bangkok's notorious traffic — use them whenever a route is covered. Tuk-tuks are fun but should be price-negotiated beforehand; metered taxis are cheap if the driver uses the meter. Longtail boats and the Chao Phraya river ferry are practical for river-adjacent sights.

## See
- Grand Palace and Wat Phra Kaew (Temple of the Emerald Buddha) — ~$15, the top must-see, strict dress code (covered shoulders/knees) enforced.
- Wat Arun (Temple of Dawn) — ~$3, striking riverside temple, especially photogenic at sunset from across the river.
- Wat Pho — ~$6, home of the giant reclining Buddha, also a birthplace of traditional Thai massage.
- Chatuchak Weekend Market — $0 entry, massive market, thousands of stalls, weekends only.
- Chinatown (Yaowarat) — $0, street food destination, especially in the evening.
- Jim Thompson House — ~$8, teak house museum showcasing Thai silk history and traditional architecture.
- Ayutthaya ruins day trip — ~$10-15, ruins of the former Siamese capital, UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- National Museum Bangkok — ~$6, one of the largest museums in Southeast Asia, Thai art and history.

## Do
- Thai cooking class — ~$25-35, widely available and popular with travelers.
- Traditional Thai massage — ~$8-15, very affordable compared to Western prices, widely available and generally high quality.
- Chao Phraya river cruise — ~$5-10 (or $30+ for dinner cruises), especially nice with skyline views at sunset.
- Day trip to the Damnoen Saduak floating market — ~$10-20 with transport, traditional boat vendors.
- Rooftop bars (e.g., in Sathorn/Silom) — ~$10-15 per drink, skyline views, mid-range splurge.
- Muay Thai match at a local stadium — ~$15-30, experience Thailand's national sport.
- Lumpini Park morning walk — $0, large green space popular for walking, paddle boats, and morning tai chi.

## Eat
- Pad thai from a street stall — ~$1-2, cheap and widely available.
- Som tam (papaya salad) — ~$1-2, spicy, tangy Northeastern Thai dish from a local vendor.
- Mango sticky rice — ~$2, classic sweet Thai dessert.
- Boat noodles (kuay teow reua) — ~$1-2, rich broth noodle soup, small servings meant to be ordered multiple times.
- Night markets (Rot Fai Market, Chinatown) — ~$2-5 per dish, combine food stalls with shopping.
- Mid-range Thai restaurants — ~$8-15, inexpensive by Western standards; upscale dining (including Michelin-starred street food stalls) is still a good value.
- Or Tor Kor Market — ~$3-6 per dish, considered one of the best fresh food/produce markets in the city.
- Tap water isn't drinkable; bottled or filtered water is cheap and everywhere.

## Drink
Beer (Chang, Singha) is cheap and widely available. Thai iced tea is a popular non-alcoholic option. Rooftop bars offer higher-end cocktails at a premium relative to street prices, but still cheaper than equivalent Western cities.

## Sleep
- Budget: hostels and guesthouses, especially around Khao San Road, 8-15 USD/night.
- Mid-range: 3-4 star hotels, 30-60 USD/night.
- Luxury: 5-star riverside hotels, 100-200+ USD/night.

## Budget
Bangkok is very budget-friendly: a traveler can manage comfortably on 25-35 USD/day including guesthouse, street food, BTS/MRT transport, and a couple of paid attractions. Mid-range comfort runs 50-80 USD/day.
