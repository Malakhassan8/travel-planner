CITY: Cairo, Egypt

## Understand
Cairo is Egypt's sprawling capital, a city of over 20 million people on the banks of the Nile. It blends ancient history (the Giza pyramids are on its outskirts), medieval Islamic architecture, and dense modern urban life. Traffic is heavy, the pace is fast, and prices are low compared to European or Southeast Asian capitals, making it a good budget destination with major historical payoff.

## Get in
Cairo International Airport (CAI) is the main gateway, about 45 minutes from downtown depending on traffic. Uber and Careem are reliable and cheap. Trains connect Cairo to Alexandria and Luxor.

## Get around
Uber/Careem are the easiest and safest options for tourists. The Cairo Metro is extremely cheap and avoids traffic but can be crowded. Walking in central Cairo (Downtown, Zamalek) is feasible; Giza and the pyramids area require a taxi or organized tour due to distance and heat.

## See
- Giza Pyramids and the Great Sphinx — ~$12, the single must-see, best visited early morning to avoid heat and crowds, extra for entering the pyramid interior.
- Egyptian Museum (Tahrir Square) — ~$10, huge collection of pharaonic artifacts including Tutankhamun's treasures. Budget 2-3 hours minimum.
- Grand Egyptian Museum (GEM) — ~$20, newer, larger museum near Giza, increasingly the primary Tutankhamun collection venue.
- Citadel of Saladin and Mosque of Muhammad Ali — ~$8, hilltop fortress with panoramic city views, striking Ottoman-style mosque.
- Khan el-Khalili bazaar — $0 entry, historic market in Islamic Cairo, good for souvenirs, spices, and people-watching; bargaining expected.
- Coptic Cairo and the Hanging Church — ~$5, old churches, quieter and historically rich, good half-day addition.
- Ibn Tulun Mosque — ~$3, one of the oldest and largest mosques in Cairo, striking spiral minaret.

## Do
- Nile dinner cruise with music and food — ~$25-35, popular tourist evening activity, mid-range cost.
- Felucca (traditional sailboat) ride at sunset on the Nile — ~$5, very affordable and relaxing.
- Sound and light show at the Pyramids in the evening — ~$15, narrated history show projected onto the monuments.
- Day trip to Saqqara and Dahshur — ~$15, older pyramid sites with far fewer tourists.
- Traditional Egyptian hammam (bathhouse) experience — ~$20, relaxing and culturally rooted.
- Whirling dervishes show at Al-Ghouri complex — ~$5-10, traditional Sufi dance performance.
- Al-Azhar Park stroll — ~$3, green space with skyline views over Islamic Cairo, relaxed pace.

## Eat
- Koshari (Egypt's national dish: rice, lentils, pasta, tomato sauce, fried onions) — ~$2-3, cheap and filling, widely available from street stalls to sit-down chains like Abou Tarek.
- Ful and taameya (fava beans and Egyptian-style falafel) for breakfast — ~$2, very cheap.
- Downtown and Zamalek mid-range restaurants — ~$10-20, Zamalek skews slightly more expensive and touristy.
- Street kushari cart or fresh juice stand — ~$1-2, quick and authentic, generally safe at busy high-turnover stalls.
- Abou El Sid — ~$15-20, upscale traditional Egyptian cuisine in a relaxed Zamalek setting.
- Fatatri Al Tahrir (fiteer, Egyptian layered pastry) — ~$5-8, sweet or savory, good for sharing.
- Nile-side seafood restaurant in Maadi — ~$12-20, fresh fish chosen before cooking.
- Bottled water is recommended with street food.

## Drink
Egypt is a Muslim-majority country; alcohol is available in hotels, some restaurants, and specific bars, but not universal. Tea (shai) and Turkish-style coffee are the default social drinks.

## Sleep
- Budget: hostels and budget hotels in Downtown Cairo, roughly 10-20 USD/night.
- Mid-range: Zamalek and Garden City hotels, 40-80 USD/night.
- Luxury: Nile-view five-star hotels (Marriott, Ritz-Carlton, Four Seasons), 150+ USD/night.

## Budget
A budget traveler can manage on roughly 25-35 USD/day including a hostel, street food, metro/local transport, and one paid attraction. Mid-range comfort (nicer hotel, taxis, restaurant meals) runs 60-100 USD/day.
