CITY: Lisbon, Portugal

## Understand
Lisbon is Portugal's hilly, coastal capital, known for pastel-colored buildings, historic trams, azulejo tilework, fado music, and being one of Western Europe's more affordable capitals. It's built across several hills along the Tagus estuary, giving many neighborhoods sweeping viewpoints (miradouros).

## Get in
Humberto Delgado Airport (LIS) is close to the city center, connected by metro, bus, and taxi/rideshare. Trains connect Lisbon to Porto, Sintra, and other regional destinations.

## Get around
The historic Tram 28 crosses many old neighborhoods and is both transport and tourist attraction (watch for pickpockets). The metro is efficient for longer distances. Central neighborhoods (Alfama, Baixa, Chiado, Bairro Alto) are hilly and best explored on foot, though the hills are steep — funiculars and elevators (like the Santa Justa Lift) help.

## See
- Belém Tower and Jerónimos Monastery — ~$6-10, UNESCO-listed Manueline architecture, Belém district, best visited together.
- Alfama district — $0, oldest neighborhood, narrow medieval streets, fado music venues, great for wandering without a fixed plan.
- São Jorge Castle — ~$10, hilltop castle with panoramic views over the city and river.
- Miradouro da Graça / Miradouro de Santa Luzia — $0, popular viewpoints, especially at sunset.
- LX Factory — $0 entry, converted industrial complex with shops, restaurants, and street art, popular with younger travelers.
- Sintra day trip (Pena Palace, Quinta da Regaleira) — ~$15-20 with train and entries, fairy-tale palaces about 40 minutes by train, very popular and often crowded.
- Carmo Convent ruins — ~$5, roofless Gothic church destroyed in the 1755 earthquake, now a small museum.
- Praça do Comércio — $0, grand riverside square rebuilt after the 1755 earthquake.

## Do
- Fado music show in Alfama or Bairro Alto — ~$15-25, an evocative traditional music experience, often paired with dinner.
- Tram 28 ride — ~$3, cheap and scenic though very crowded at peak times — early morning is better.
- Day trip to Sintra or the coastal town of Cascais — ~$5-20 depending on entries.
- Pastel de nata tasting — ~$1.50 each, Lisbon's famous custard tart, best known from Pastéis de Belém.
- National Azulejo (Tile) Museum visit — ~$5, dedicated to Portugal's famous decorative ceramic tiles.
- Cais do Sodré riverside walk — $0, relaxed waterfront promenade, good for unwinding.
- Feira da Ladra flea market — $0 entry, historic flea market held Tuesdays and Saturdays.

## Eat
- Pastéis de nata (custard tarts) — ~$1.50 each, a must, widely available and cheap.
- Bacalhau (salt cod) dishes — ~$8-12, a Portuguese staple, prepared dozens of different ways.
- Time Out Market Lisboa — ~$8-15 per dish, a curated food hall with stalls from well-known local chefs, good for sampling variety in one place.
- Tascas (small traditional restaurants) — ~$8-12, offer affordable, hearty set menus (menu do dia).
- Bifana sandwich — ~$3-4, marinated pork sandwich, classic cheap Lisbon lunch.
- Grilled sardines (especially in summer) — ~$6-10, classic Lisbon street food, popular during festivals.
- Queijo da Serra and bread at a local market — ~$4-6, traditional cheese, good for a light budget meal.

## Drink
Ginjinha (cherry liqueur) is a popular, cheap local specialty, often sold from small hole-in-the-wall shops, around $1.50 a shot. Portuguese wine, especially Vinho Verde, is good value; Port wine (technically from Porto) is widely available too.

## Sleep
- Budget: hostels in Baixa or Alfama, 15-25 USD/night.
- Mid-range: boutique hotels in Chiado or Príncipe Real, 60-100 USD/night.
- Luxury: five-star riverside or historic hotels, 180+ USD/night.

## Budget
Budget travelers can manage 35-50 USD/day with hostel stays, walking/tram, tasca meals, and pastel de nata snacks. Mid-range comfort runs 80-120 USD/day.
