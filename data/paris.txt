CITY: Paris, France

## Understand
Paris is France's capital, world-famous for its art, architecture, fashion, and food. It's a compact, walkable city divided by the Seine into Left Bank and Right Bank, with 20 numbered arrondissements spiraling out from the center. Expect higher costs than much of Europe, especially for accommodation and sit-down dining.

## Get in
Charles de Gaulle (CDG) and Orly (ORY) are the main airports; RER trains and buses connect both to the city center. Eurostar connects Paris to London via the Gare du Nord. The Paris Metro network is extensive.

## Get around
The Metro is fast, cheap, and covers the whole city; a Navigo Easy card or day passes are cost-effective for multi-day stays. Central Paris (Louvre to Notre-Dame to Latin Quarter) is very walkable. Velib' bike-share is popular for longer distances.

## See
- Eiffel Tower: iconic, book tickets in advance to skip long queues; sunset/night views are especially popular.
- Louvre Museum: world's largest art museum, home to the Mona Lisa; needs at least half a day, book timed entry.
- Notre-Dame Cathedral: still under restoration after the 2019 fire but the exterior and surrounding Île de la Cité are worth a visit.
- Musée d'Orsay: Impressionist and Post-Impressionist masterpieces in a converted train station, often less crowded than the Louvre.
- Montmartre and Sacré-Cœur: hilltop basilica, artist square, and panoramic views; touristy but atmospheric.
- Arc de Triomphe and Champs-Élysées: monument plus a major shopping avenue.
- Palace of Versailles: half-day or full-day trip outside the city, opulent former royal residence and gardens.

## Do
- Seine river cruise (bateaux-mouches) for a relaxed way to see major sights, especially nice at night.
- Wander Le Marais for boutique shopping, historic architecture, and a strong food scene.
- Catch a cabaret show (e.g., Moulin Rouge) for a splurge-worthy evening.
- Picnic in Luxembourg Gardens or along the Seine, a very Parisian, budget-friendly activity.

## Eat
- Boulangeries for cheap, high-quality breakfast (croissants, pain au chocolat) — often the best value food in the city.
- Bistros for classic French dishes (steak frites, coq au vin, onion soup); prices vary widely by neighborhood.
- Markets like Marché des Enfants Rouges offer affordable, varied lunch options.
- Fine dining and Michelin-starred restaurants are plentiful but expensive; book well ahead.

## Drink
- Wine bars are a core part of Parisian culture; a glass of wine with cheese is a classic, affordable evening activity.
- Café culture: sitting at a sidewalk café with coffee is inexpensive and a quintessential Paris experience.

## Sleep
- Budget: hostels and budget hotels, especially near Gare du Nord or in the outer arrondissements, 30-60 USD/night.
- Mid-range: 3-star hotels in central arrondissements, 100-180 USD/night.
- Luxury: iconic hotels (Ritz, Le Meurice) and boutique 5-star properties, 300+ USD/night.

## Budget
Budget travelers can manage 60-80 USD/day with hostel stays, metro passes, boulangerie meals, and free/cheap sights (churches, parks, some museums on free days). Mid-range comfort runs 150-220 USD/day.
